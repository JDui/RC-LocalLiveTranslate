# ADR-0009: Sentence endpoints, reviewed speaker attribution and verified language preference

Date: 2026-10-29. Status: software implementation and host regression coverage; human speech and ARM64 acceptance pending. Revised after the independent Astra review on the same date.

## Sentence endpoints

Keep the fixed SenseVoice, Silero, CAM++ and Hy-MT2 weights. Silero uses 0.28-second minimum silence and a 10-second aggressive long-speech threshold. An explicit 160,000-sample frontend limit remains a resource fallback, not a semantic sentence claim.

In addition to pauses, speculative ASR can propose an endpoint during continuous speech. The same sentence-ending token prefix must appear in two distinct growing recognition windows, with token onset drift <= 0.18 seconds. Raw token concatenation must agree with the displayed ASR text after whitespace removal; malformed, nonfinite, nonmonotonic or out-of-window timestamps are rejected. Common titles, decimal points and ellipses do not create endpoints. Require following-token audio and at least 0.8 seconds before the cut. No post-ITN character index or proportional text timing is used.

The endpoint uses the following token onset minus one 60-ms CTC frame of context, rounded down to the native 512-sample grid. The frontend accepts it only for its still-current segment and a retained complete audio tail. It flushes the prefix to final ASR, resets the native VAD time origin to the cut, and replays every retained tail sample. Existing input residual samples stay pending. Final ASR sends the completed sentence to translation without waiting for speaker identity. Stop, pause and discontinuities flush all remaining speech. Native sub-window input is completed with zeros before flush, and that padding is removed from returned speech.

This is model-supported sentence endpoint detection, not perfect semantic understanding or phoneme alignment. Missing/mistaken punctuation, hesitations, rapid turns and imperfect CTC onset positions can still miss or misplace boundaries. Existing Windows TTS observations with the real fixed model include omitted sentence punctuation and a period transcribed as a comma; see `.tools/astra-fixes-real-token-observations.json`. Those cases fall back to pauses or the resource limit. Unpunctuated continuous speech is not guaranteed to split into linguistic sentences. Model and endpoint quality must be measured on human recordings; synthetic orchestration tests cannot establish it.

## Speaker identity

At about 0.8 seconds, `preview` may reserve a provisional label or match a confirmed voice. It never trains an established template or increments accepted final evidence. Complete utterance evidence is re-evaluated once, and contradictory, invalid or ambiguous evidence can correct or withdraw the preview.

Provisional reservations are separate from committed identities. Withdrawal or eviction releases an unconfirmed reservation; it cannot exhaust the lifetime identity quota. A label becomes committed only with accepted final evidence. Committed historical identities, including merged labels, are never reused; at most 16 are committed per session. Reservations plus active identities also fit that finite label space. Matching retains acoustic anchors, including anchors of merged voices; template updates additionally require centroid cosine >= 0.75 and anchor support >= 0.65. Language and text never participate in acoustic matching. These are initial software rules, not measured human-speaker accuracy.

Final speaker jobs lost to overload or policy changes invalidate a monotonic preview epoch. A conflated signal withdraws pending labels in the speaker worker, and queued obsolete previews are skipped. Synchronous embedding must return before withdrawal. Shutdown withdraws remaining previews. Final evidence and confirmed identities survive optional-work degradation.

## Language preference and asynchronous revisions

Each confirmed voice retains its chronologically earliest reliable automatic language observation (>=19,200 samples and native language-score margin >=0.6). A 0.6 log-score prior proposes that language only for close later candidates. A clear competing language wins immediately. Unknown tags, invalid scores or absent acoustic identity preserve auto. The score and duration gates are uncalibrated starting parameters.

The initial final transcript and translation always use auto immediately. Both speaker completion orders are supported. Late final speaker assignments, merges, splits or a newly available earlier preference wake bounded review in the ASR worker. The proposal is never directly relabelled or routed: a per-stream `rc_language_hint` requests an actual second recognition hypothesis from the same model. The first auto pass and other streams remain unchanged.

Accept a preferred hypothesis only if the model returns that language, both texts are compatible with it, and normalized letter/digit content is identical. Numeric expressions additionally preserve signs, decimal/grouping separators, ratios, currency and percentages; a near edit-distance match cannot remove negation or alter a number. Script checks only reject incompatible hypotheses: they cannot assign languages or establish preferences. Latin-only or predominantly Latin speech cannot be relabelled Chinese/Japanese/Korean and silently lose translation. Clear Japanese kana and Korean hangul cannot be forced into incompatible initial languages. Shared Han text and numeric utterances remain ambiguous; even a verified preference is not ground-truth language evidence.

An accepted hypothesis publishes a higher transcript revision with the verified language and schedules the resulting route. It preserves the automatic transcription, including its punctuation and capitalization, rather than replacing content with a forced decode. If acoustic correction invalidates that choice, review restores auto or another verified language. Translation work carries a revision ticket: obsolete queued work is skipped and results from obsolete in-flight work cannot overwrite the revision. A synchronous JNI call is allowed to return; no unsafe interruption of native execution is attempted.

The ASR worker retains 256 recent final metadata records, PCM for at most eight final segments (at most about 80 seconds of maximum-sized chunks), and at most two preferred decode attempts per record. Cached results can be reused; if audio or review budget is unavailable, keep/restore auto. Final recognition has priority over optional review and partial recognition. At stop, reviews drain after the speaker worker finishes, within the existing shutdown cancellation policy. These finite bounds intentionally prefer auto under sustained overload rather than retaining unlimited speech or delaying initial translation.

## Native preparation and evidence

The `rc-sensevoice-language-scores-v1` adapter reads five actual first-frame language-tag logits without changing them. It requires finite valid candidates and a supported first-frame full-vocabulary winner; Kotlin also checks agreement with the automatic tag. The additional `rc-sensevoice-language-hint-v2` patch only enables optional per-stream second-pass language input. Both patches are fixed against the pinned sherpa commit. Preparation is idempotent, rejects incompatible source changes, and includes all local patch/helper sources beside native licenses. Build and APK verification require both markers and source assets. Model identities, dependency versions and weight licenses do not change.

PCM, embeddings, logits and recognition hypotheses are not added to disk storage, exports or production logs. All review state is session-local and cleared at shutdown. Host synthetic tests cover the independent-review reproductions, both completion orders, content/route safety, obsolete translations, reservations, and sample-preserving semantic replay. Native compilation and real-model TTS token observations remain distinct from Android/human acceptance. No device latency, cross-language human identity accuracy, overlapping-speaker recovery or calibrated confidence claim is made.
